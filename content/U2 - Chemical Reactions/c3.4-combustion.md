# C3.4 - Combustion

## What is combustion?

**combustion:** reaction where a fuel (hydrocarbon) quickly burns in oxygen to produce heat and light (seen as flame)

- commonly called burning
- common fuels: coal, propane, gasoline
- chemical energy converted into thermal and light energy
- fuels are composed of organic compounds, which are typically hydrocarbons

**hydrocarbon:** compound that contains carbon and hydrogen

- chemical formula format: $\text C_x \text H_y$
- examples: methane ($\text{CH}_4$), propane ($\text C_3 \text H_8$)
- most hydrocarbons are obtained from fossil fuels: coal, oil, natural gas
- gasoline is a mixture of various hydrocarbons

## Types of Combustion

- complete combustion
- incomplete combustion

## Complete Combustion

**complete combustion:** combustion of a hydrocarbon in lots of oxygen to produce carbon dioxide, water, and energy

*General Equation:* hydrocarbon + oxygen &rarr; carbon dioxide + water

- occurs with a plentiful supply of oxygen
- burns w/ clean, blue flame
- produces heat and light

*Example: Combustion of liquid octane (component of gasoline)*

$$
\begin{equation*}
2\text C_8 \text H_{18}(l) + 25\text O_2(g) \rightarrow 16\text{CO}_2(g) + 18\text H_2 \text O(g)
\end{equation*}
$$

## Incomplete Combustion

**incomplete combustion:** combustion of hydrocarbon in limited oxygen to produce carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide, soot, water, and energy

*General Equation:* hydrocarbon + oxygen &rarr; carbon dioxide + water + carbon monoxide + carbon (soot)

- occurs w/ limited, insufficient supply of oxygen (more common)
- usually occurs in an enclosed space, like car operation or BBQs in garages
- **soot:** fine black particles of carbon thrown in the air
- burns with a sooty, yellow flame
- **:warning: DANGER:** carbon monoxide and soot too

### Dangers of Soot

- Harmful (long-term) for breathing
- Damages the lungs
- Causes respiratory problems like asthma

### :warning: Dangers of Carbon Monoxide

- a.k.a. "silent killer"
- poisonous gas w/ no colour or smell
- prevents oxygen from binding w/ the hemoglobin in the blood
  - blocks cells from recieving oxygen
- best way to protect against CO: a CO detector
