# C4.4 - Empirical and Molecular Formulas

## Empirical vs. Molecular Formula

**empirical formula:** formula of molecule whose subscripts are reduced to the smallest whole number ratio

*Examples*

|Compound|Molecular Formula|Empirical Formula|
|-|-|-|
|acetylene|$\text C_2 \text H_2$|$\text{CH}$|
|benzene|$\text C_6 \text H_6$|$\text{CH}$|
|carbon dioxide|$\text{CO}_2$|$\text{CO}_2$|
|ribose|$\text C_5 \text H_{10} \text O_5$|$\text{CH}_2 \text O$|

## Find Empirical Formula from Composition

**Steps**

1. Convert percent composition of each element as grams of an exact 100 g sample
   1. i.e. 12.65% of carbon would be 12.65 g of carbon
2. Convert mass to moles using molar mass
   1. use $n=\frac mM$
   2. **ROUNDING RULE:** Round to only a maximum of 4 digits
3. Find the subscript of each atom by dividing each atom's mole with the *smallest mole value* found
   1. If the decimal place of the number is between 0.15 and 0.85, **do not round to nearest whole**
      1. round to nearest tenth or nearest hundreth for cases like 0.25 (1/4)
   2. Otherwise, round to nearest whole
4. If decimal subscripts exist, multiply all subscripts with a whole number that will make all values close to a whole number
   1. find the smallest value that makes all numbers a number whose decimal place is not between 0.15 to 0.85
   2. round that value to the nearest whole
5. Write the formula. Typically carbon is first in a molecular compound. List metals first if in compound

**Example**

Given: 60.0% C, 4.5% H, 35.5% O where $M_\text C = 12.01 \text{ g/mol}$, $M_\text H = 1.01 \text{ g/mol}$, $M_\text O = 16.00 \text{ g/mol}$

Required: empirical formula

*Steps shown on side*

let $a$ be # of atoms / molecule

$$
\begin{gather*}
\: m_\text C = 60.0 \text{ g}, \: m_\text H = 4.5 \text{ g}, \: m_\text O = 35.5 \text{ g} \tag{1} \\
n_\text{C} = \frac{m_\text{C}}{M_\text{C}} = \frac{60.0 \text{ g}}{12.01 \text{ g/mol}} = 4.9958 \text{ mol} \tag{2} \\
n_\text{C} = \frac{m_\text{C}}{M_\text{C}} = \frac{4.5 \text{ g}}{1.01 \text{ g/mol}} = 4.4554 \text{ mol} \\
n_\text{C} = \frac{m_\text{C}}{M_\text{C}} = \frac{35.5 \text{ g}}{16.00 \text{ g/mol}} = 2.2188 \text{ mol} \\ \\
\text{smallest mole value: } 2.2188 \text{ mol} \tag{3} \\
a_\text{C} = \frac{4.9958 \text{ mol}}{2.2188 \text{ mol}} = 2.25 \\
a_\text{H} = \frac{4.4554 \text{ mol}}{2.2188 \text{ mol}} = 2.00 = 2 \\
a_\text{O} = \frac{2.2188 \text{ mol}}{2.2188 \text{ mol}} = 1 \\ \\
\text{incomplete formula: } \text C_{2.25} \text H_2 \text O \tag{4} \\
4(\text C_{2.25} \text H_2 \text O) = \text C_9 \text H_8 \text O_4 \\ \\
\text{empirical formula: } \text C_9 \text H_8 \text O_4 \tag{5}
\end{gather*}
$$

## Find Molecular Formula from Empirical

1. Required values: empirical formula (EF), molar mass of elements and EF, *molar mass of substance*
2. Find the multiplier ($x$)
   1. multiplier = molar mass of substance / molar mass of empirical formula
   2. round to nearest whole
3. Multiply empirical formula by the multiplier

*Formulas*

$$
\begin{gather*}
x = \frac{M_\text{comp}}{M_{EF}} \\
MF = x(EF)
\end{gather*}
$$
