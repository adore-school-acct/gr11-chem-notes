# C5.4 - Percentage Yield

## Theoretical Yield

*Note that variables used are made up by me*

**theoretical yield ($y_t$):** the mass of the product determined through stoichiometry

- achieved if 100% of the limiting reactant converts into products
- rarely achieved in real life

## Actual Yield

**actual yield ($y_a$):** mass of the product collected during an experiment

*Usually less than the theoretical yield*

### Reasons for Less Actual Yield than Theoretical Yield

- Nature of the Reaction
  - reaction may be reversible, so a competing side reaction may occur
- Experimental Procedure
  - small quantities of reactants may be inevitably lost
    - splattering of chemicals from heating or during reaction
    - filtering usually doesn't filter out all of the product
- Impurities in Chemicals
  - chemicals used are rarely 100% pure
  - that means the mass of the actual reactant is usually less than the measured mass (due to impurities)
- Competing Side Reactions
  - competing reactions may prevent the reactant from fully converting into the desired product
  - i.e., burning magnesium in air (oxygen) to produce magnesium oxide may produce little bits of magnesium nitride too since nitrogen is also in the air
  
## Percentage Yield

**percentage yield ($y_p$):** ratio between the actual yield and the theoretical yield as a percentage

let $y_r$ also be yield ratio (not expressed as percentage)

$$
\begin{gather*}
\text{percentage yield} = \frac{\text{actual yield}}{\text{theoretical yield}} \cdot 100\% \\
y_p = \frac{y_a}{y_t} \cdot 100\% \\
y_r = \frac{y_a}{y_t}
\end{gather*}
$$

### Reasons for Calculating Percentage Yield

- gives chemists info about how much product to expect
- to analyze the efficiency of the reaction
- to compare % yield in different conditions to find maximum efficiency

### Determine Percentage Yield

1. Determine theoretical yield using stoichiometry
   1. If limiting reactant is not told, must figure it out first
2. Actual yield is determined using experiments, will be given during a test
3. Use equation above
