# C5.3 - Limiting Reactant

## Stoichiometric Amounts

**stoichiometric amount:** amounts of reactants are in the same proportion as the (mole) ratio of reactants

- stoichiometric amounts of reactants are used up completely after reaction
- no reactants should remain after reaction is complete

i.e. 40 mol potassium and 10 mol oxygen producing 20 mol potassium oxide is in stoichiometric amounts because...

$$
\begin{gather*}
4\text K + \text O_2 \rightarrow 2\text K_2 \text O \\
\Downarrow \\
40\text{ mol K} + 10\text{ mol O}_2 \rightarrow 20\text{ mol K}_2\text O
\end{gather*}
$$

However, 60 mol potassium and 10 mol oxygen producing 20 mol potassium oxide is in *non-stoichiometric amounts*, since only 40 mol potassium is needed.

*20 mol potassium left over and 20 mol potassium oxide created*

## Limiting and Excess Reactants (Reagents)

**limiting reactant/reagent:** the reactant that is completely used up during reaction

**excess reactant/reagent:** the reactant that is left over after the reaction

One limiting reactant in reaction, 1 or more excess reactant

For 60 mol potassium reacting with 10 mol oxygen to produce 20 mol potassium oxide

- potassium is excess reactant &mdash; need: 40 mol, have: 60 mol
- oxygen is limiting reactant &mdash; need and have 10 mol

## Find Limiting Reactant in Chemical Equation

Example: $4\text K + \text O_2 \rightarrow 2\text K_2 \text O$ where we have 99.00 g of potassium and 15.00 g of oxygen

1. Balance chemical equation, if necessary
2. Convert masses of all reactants into moles, if mass is given instead of moles
   1. i.e. $n_\text K = \frac{99.00 \text{ g}}{39.10 \text{ g/mol}} = 2.531969 \text{ mol}$
   2. i.e. $n_{\text O_2} = \frac{15.00\text{ g}}{32.00\text{ g/mol}} = 0.46875\text{ mol}$
3. Divide the moles of each reactant by its coefficient
   1. ex. $n_\text{K in 4} = \frac{2.531969 \text{ mol}} 4 = 0.63299225 \text{ mol} = 0.6330\text{ mol}$
   2. ex. $n_{\text O_2\text{ in 1}} = \frac{0.46875\text{ mol}} 1 = 0.46875\text{ mol} = 0.4688\text{ mol}$
4. The smallest value above is the limiting reactant. Express the reactant using its value in moles ($n_{LR}$)
   1. ex. $\text O_2$ has the smallest value of 0.4688 mol
   2. Therefore, oxygen is the limiting reactant
   3. $n_{LR} = 0.4688 \text{ mol}$
