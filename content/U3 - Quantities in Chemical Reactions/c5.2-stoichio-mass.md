# C5.2 - Stoichiometric Mass

## Stoichiometric Mass Calculations

How to determine mass of required substance from given substance's mass and equation.

Example: Find mass of lithium hydroxide from 880.0 g of carbon dioxide 

$$
\begin{equation*}
\text{CO}_2(g) + 2\text{LiOH}(s) \rightarrow \text{Li}_2\text{CO}_3(s) + 2\text H_2\text O(l)
\end{equation*}
$$

1. Find molar mass of given and required substance
   1. i.e. molar mass of carbon dioxide: 44.01 g/mol
   2. i.e. molar mass of lithium hydroxide: 23.95 g/mol
2. Convert mass of given substance to moles
   1. ex. $n_{\text{CO}_2}=\frac{880.0 \text{ g}}{44.01 \text{ g/mol}} = 19.995456 \text{ mol}$
   2. Do not round to nearest # of sig figs, keep 4-6 decimal places
3. Use mole ratio to determine moles of required substance
   1. i.e., mole ratio = $\frac{2 \text{ mol LiOH}}{1 \text{ mol CO}_2}$
   2. ex. $n_\text{LiOH} = (19.995456 \cancel{\text{ mol CO}_2})(\frac{2 \text{ mol LiOH}}{\cancel{1 \text{ mol CO}_2}}) = 39.990912 \text{ mol LiOH}$
4. Convert moles of required substance back into mass
   1. ex. $m_\text{LiOH} = (39.990912 \text{ mol})(23.95 \text{ g/mol}) = 957.7823424 \text{ g} = 957.8 \text{ g}$
5. Write a concluding statement, if necessary
   1. ex. $\therefore$ The mass of lithium hydroxide should be 957.8 g
